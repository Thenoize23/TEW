MixHaven vs The Engineer's Workpad — organizing your studio isn't the same as finishing tracks

An honest comparison from people with an obvious bias, written so it's useful anyway.

Full disclosure first: we make The Engineer's Workpad, a finishing tracker for producers. So instead of pretending neutrality, this page does something more useful — it tells you exactly where MixHaven genuinely wins, because in its territory it's a genuinely well-made app, and where a finishing tracker earns its existence. If we're wrong about the trade-offs, you'll see it in the table.

What MixHaven is actually great at

MixHaven's pitch is "turn studio chaos into creative control," and the parts of that promise it keeps are the Apple-native ones:

  • Real native apps, offline by default. macOS, iPad and iPhone, with your files stored locally and iCloud sync only for what you explicitly share. If you distrust web apps with your studio data, this is the strongest privacy stance in this category.
  • Audio versioning inside the organizer. Playback built in, mix iterations compared side by side, time-coded comments on annotated waveforms. Your v3-vs-v7 question gets answered in the app.
  • Asset tracking beyond audio. Samples, stems, even contracts, tagged and attached to the project they belong to. A real answer to "where is everything for this song?"
  • A clean status dashboard. Projects filtered by Idea, Mixed, Scheduled, Released — a genuine overview of a busy studio.

If you live entirely in the Apple ecosystem and what you want is a private, local, beautifully built home for your projects and their files, MixHaven is a legitimate choice and you can stop reading.

The problem it doesn't solve

Look at those statuses again: Idea → Mixed → Scheduled → Released. Those are catalog states — they tell you where a track sits on the shelf, not what's left to do inside it. The jump from "Idea" to "Mixed" is where producers lose months, and a status field crosses it in one click, whenever you decide to click it. That's the same one-label trap as a Notion checkbox that says "Mix ☐": the label hides a dozen real steps, so updating it is a judgment call, and judgment calls are exactly what stall tracks (see: when is a song actually finished?).

The second limit is the door policy: MixHaven is Apple-only. macOS, iPad, iPhone. If your studio machine runs Windows — like a large share of home producers — or your phone runs Android, there is no MixHaven for you at any price. And speaking of price: the site says free download, 7-day trial, then "a subscription or a one-time purchase" — with the actual numbers not published on the page. You find out inside the app.

The actual decision rule

It's not "which tool is better" — they're pointed at different problems:

  • All-Apple studio, and your pain is scattered files, lost versions and no overview → MixHaven. Genuinely. A finishing tracker doesn't store your stems and never will.
  • Any platform, and your pain is tracks circling at 80% forever → a tool where the finishing method is built in, so "what's actually left on this track?" always has a named, countable answer.

And if you're on Windows or Android, the decision has been made for you — one of these two tools runs on your machine and the other doesn't.

The test that settles it in five minutes: open your current project and try to answer "what exactly is left before this track is done?" If your answer is a status ("it's in mixing"), you have an organizer. If your answer is a list of named steps, you have a method. Buy the one you're missing.
